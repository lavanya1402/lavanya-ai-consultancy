For teachers · Checklist

Before any AI output reaches a student

Run these checks on every worksheet, explanation, question or rubric drafted with AI. It takes a few minutes and catches most problems.

  • Facts: every fact, date, formula and definition matches your textbook or a source you trust.
  • Level: vocabulary and difficulty suit your class, not a general reader.
  • Syllabus: nothing is outside the current syllabus, and nothing deleted from it has crept back.
  • Answers: you have solved each question yourself; the answer key and marking scheme are correct.
  • One right answer: multiple-choice questions have exactly one defensible answer.
  • Bias and respect: examples, names and situations are fair to all students.
  • Sources: any quotation or statistic has a real source you have opened.
  • Privacy: no student names, marks or personal details were entered into the AI tool.
  • Your judgement: you would be comfortable putting your own name on it.

How to choose an AI project when you're not technical

A good first project is small, comes from your own subject, and has a result you can check.

  1. Start from a task in your field. An HR student might screen job descriptions; a logistics student might explain delivery delays.
  2. Name the user. Who would use this, and what would they do differently?
  3. Keep the input small. One kind of document or one dataset you can actually get.
  4. Decide how you will check it. Ten examples where you already know the right answer is a good start.
  5. Write down the limits early. Where could it be wrong, and who should make the final decision?
  6. Plan the demo. If you can't show it working in two minutes, make it smaller.

What to write in a project README

The README is the first page anyone sees on GitHub. Copy this outline into your repository and fill in each part in your own words.

Recruiters read the first three sections. Make them clear.

# Project name

One sentence: what it does and for whom.

## The problem
Who has this problem, and why it matters.

## What I built
What the user does, and what they get back.
Add one or two screenshots.

## How it works
The steps, the tools used, and why you chose them.

## How I checked it
The examples you tested and what happened.

## Limitations
Where it can be wrong. Who should make the final decision.

## My contribution
What you did yourself. In a group, who did what.

## How to run it
Setup steps someone else can follow.
