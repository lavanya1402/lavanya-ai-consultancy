A note from our Founder & CEO

Why Lavanya AI Consultancy exists

I have spent fifty years watching the world change, and twenty-five years helping people learn through that change.

I grew up in a world of inland letters and postcards, when a phone call was an event. I watched that world give way to telephones, then pagers, then mobile phones in every pocket. Each wave of technology asked my generation to unlearn and relearn. Now artificial intelligence has arrived, and for the first time, children are inheriting a technology that can think.

My own path has followed every one of these shifts.

I began as a scientist. As a gold medallist in conservation sciences and a research assistant at IISc Bangalore, I designed research methodologies, collected field data and analysed it statistically. Research taught me the discipline of evidence: never trust a conclusion you cannot test.

Then I became a teacher. For nearly two decades, from 2001 to 2020, I taught Science and Mathematics to school students and Statistics and Research Methodology to college and university learners. I am UGC-NET qualified and a trained teacher with a B.Ed. I have set question papers late into the night, and I have seen what one good teacher can do for a child, and what an overloaded one cannot.

Then I became a builder. My love for statistics led me to data science and machine learning, an intensive year-long programme with Great Learning, and more than 50 certifications in AI. I did not stop at learning it. I built it: Generative AI applications, retrieval systems, and, from 2024 to 2026, Agentic AI systems with guardrails and human approval at every step. Today I design AI-integrated university curricula and have taught more than 800 university students and 100 working professionals how AI really works.

Then I became a bridge. My move into AI was not a straight line. After two decades in classrooms, I stepped far outside my comfort zone and started again, learning to code, to model data and to build AI systems from the ground up. I know how complex that climb feels, and how much courage it takes to begin. That is exactly the ease I now bring to others: to school children meeting AI for the first time, to non-technical learners who fear it is not for them, and to technical professionals moving into a new field. Every stratum of society deserves a way in, and I have walked that road myself.

Scientist, teacher, builder, bridge. Together, these lives led me to one question: what must education give children today so they are ready for a world we cannot yet see? The answer is different for developed and developing nations, which is why I chose to connect globally, starting with India, Singapore and the UAE.

I have learned that technology is a double-edged knife. Using it is easy; using it rightly is a responsibility. So at Lavanya AI Consultancy, we hold three principles:

  • The teacher decides. AI drafts; a human always approves.
  • Right knowledge at the right age. Young children need foundations and imagination, not screens. AI enters gradually, with guardrails.
  • Enhance, never replace. AI must strengthen a child's natural creativity and capability, never dull them.

Fifty years have taught me that technology never reaches a plateau. It rises every day, and education must rise with it, wisely and ethically.

We exist so that as artificial intelligence grows, human intelligence grows stronger.
